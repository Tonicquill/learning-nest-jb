 EDUSKY PRIVATE SCHOOL Strategy Package May 2026 This document is a strategic action plan for Edusky Private School, organised by dependency order — what must happen before what. Each layer unlocks the next. Skipping a layer means the one above it will not hold. The recommendations are grounded in direct observation of the school’s operations, its students, its staff, and its competitive environment over a period of weeks — not in a surface-level web audit. Where the document references specific student interests, facility conditions, or community dynamics, it is because those things have been seen firsthand. Where Edusky Stands Today Edusky Private School is operating at single-digit student occupancy. The owner, Dr. Sean Ng, has acknowledged that leadership involvement has been insufficient and that the school is, in his words, at rock bottom. Chinese investors have visited the premises, responded positively to the physical space, but declined to commit at current enrollment levels. Meanwhile, Wilford Private School — located on the same street, No. 65 to Edusky’s No. 71 — is growing, has a professional online presence, and runs dual-curriculum programs. This is the honest starting position. It is also a position with more assets than it appears. What Edusky Actually Has A physical school with real facilities. Computer Lab, Science Lab, classrooms, hall. The building exists and is well-maintained. Investors noticed. Real students with real stories. Small cohort, but each student is known individually — their strengths, their interests, their lives outside school. One student has a remarkable movie collection. Another is deeply involved with animals. Several are on the spectrum or have ADHD. These are not statistics; they are people the school knows by name. Teachers who are already creating content. The TikTok account is active and decently updated. Teachers are posting to Facebook and Instagram. The effort exists — it lacks direction, strategy, and integration, but the willingness is there. Multiple service lines that nobody can see. According to company listings and direct conversations, Edusky Learning Academy Sdn Bhd (incorporated 2014) offers homeschool programs, corporate training, IELTS preparation, Cambridge English, and tuition classes. There is also a language center in Medini. None of this is visible online. None of it feeds the others. A website, a domain, social media accounts, and a company registration. The infrastructure exists. It is thin, fragmented, and sometimes broken — but it exists. What Is Broken The website (edusky.com.my) has only 3 pages: Home, About Us, and Facilities. No curriculum, fees, teacher profiles, admissions process, or blog. The testimonials section has a broken entry — Linda K’s quote repeats another parent’s verbatim. Two different email addresses appear across the site. The brand is split between “Edusky Learning Academy” (old Sdn Bhd name, old Facebook page with 2,200 likes, Google Business listing, dead 2018 WordPress blog) and “Edusky Private School” (current website, new Facebook page with 15 likes, Instagram, TikTok). This fragmentation dilutes an already small footprint. Instagram has 280 followers but follows 691 accounts. 626 posts with low engagement. No Instagram Highlights, no business analytics. A YouTube channel exists under “Edusky Learning Academy” but is not linked from the website or any social media. It is invisible. Google Business lists the school under the old name with no reviews, no updated photos, and incorrect information. An old WordPress.com blog (eduskyhomeschool.wordpress.com) with a single post from June 2018 is still indexed by Google, creating a dead-end impression. What the Competitor Has Wilford Private School, at 65 Jalan Adda 3/1, has a professional website (wilford.edu.my) with curriculum details, class breakdowns by age, teacher profiles, and coding programs. They run a dual curriculum (Malaysia + Cambridge), display MOE accreditation prominently, offer IGCSE preparation, maintain active Facebook, Instagram, and YouTube channels, and publish content showing STEM projects and student activities. They accept students aged 2–19. This is not about whether Wilford is better. It is about the fact that a parent comparing the two online will choose Wilford because Wilford gives them more information. Information builds trust. Layer 2: Operational Infrastructure Blocked by Layer 1. For current parents plus interested prospects. Use for school announcements, event invitations, and value-added content (parenting tips, education articles). Build the roster now so camps can launch in Layer 5. Layer 3: Content Engine Blocked by Layer 2. Media consent forms must be signed. Content calendar must exist. SkyPoints must be ready. The Student Production System Every student carries a production studio in their pocket. The school’s job is not to create content for them but to channel what they already do into something that benefits them and the school. ★ ACTION: Launch Student Clubs (Start with 3) Media Club. 4–6 students. Roles: Editor, Photographer, Videographer, Writer. They photograph events, film activities, write blog posts, and produce social media content. One teacher advises (30 minutes/week oversight, not execution). Students shoot content on their own phones. This club is the school’s content team. Community Service Club. The formal vehicle for all CSR and volunteer work. Students plan, organise, and execute service projects. Teacher supervises. This club generates the highest-value marketing content because it shows Edusky students doing things other schools’ students do not do. One creative club (Art Club or Debate Club). Art Club produces visible output: school posters, event flyers, website illustrations, reception displays. Debate Club produces competitive entries: inter-school competitions that put Edusky’s name in front of other schools’ parents. Choose based on student interest. ★ ACTION: Student Content Program &quot;Life Outside Edusky&quot; series. Bi-weekly or monthly. One student profiled: their hobbies, passions, collections, pets, family life. Go beyond achievements. The student with the movie collection is a story about passion and taste. The student with many cats is a story about responsibility. The goal is to show that Edusky sees the whole child. Format: 60-second video for TikTok/Reels + longer photo essay for website/Instagram carousel. &quot;Day in the Life&quot; takeovers. One student takes over the school’s Instagram or TikTok for a day. Films arrival, favourite class, lunch, after-school. Unscripted. These perform extremely well because they are authentic. Student-created tutorials. Students teach something on camera: how they memorise sifir, how to fold origami, how they organise their school bag. Peer-to-peer content for TikTok. Phone Licence system. Students who join the Media Club or content team earn a physical card/badge granting phone use during designated content-creation time. Requires completing a 10-minute digital responsibility briefing and signing a student media agreement. Can be revoked for misuse. This reframes phones from distraction to tool. ★ ACTION: SkyPoints Incentive Launch Announce at assembly. Show the points table, the reward tiers, the leaderboard. Make it exciting. Let students sign up for clubs on the spot. Points structure. Published article: 20pts. Photo/video used on social media: 10pts. Content with 50+ engagement: 15pts bonus. Club officer role: 30pts/semester. External event representation: 25pts. CSR volunteering: 20pts. 4-week contribution streak: 25pts bonus. Reward tiers. Monthly announcement of top 3 contributors. ★ ACTION: School Newspaper (Issue 1 within 6 weeks) Target: first issue published within 6 weeks of club launch. It does not need to be perfect. The Umbrella Question Edusky already operates multiple service lines: primary school, language training, corporate training, tuition, IELTS preparation. There is a language center in Medini. These are currently invisible, disconnected, and cannibalising each other’s potential. The question Dr. Sean needs to answer is: why haven’t these been consolidated under one brand? The four most common honest answers are: “I never thought of it,” “They have different partners or obligations,” “Some aren’t ready to be public,” or “I was too busy running each one to think about the whole.” Each answer points to a different pace of consolidation, but the strategic case is the same. Why This Matters for Investors A group structure presents a portfolio, not a single struggling school. Investors invest in systems, not classrooms. It creates multiple revenue entry points. An investor might not fund a primary school but might fund a language center or training arm. It demonstrates strategic thinking from the founder. Investors back founders who see structure, not just survival. It shifts the conversation from “Can this school survive?” to “How fast can this group scale?” Case Study: Stellar Education Group Also based in Johor Bahru. Founded in 2016 with a single preschool. The founder, Daniel Loh, personally does thought leadership and community building. Stellar started from a comparable position to Edusky and grew through brand consolidation and community presence, not just enrollment. ★ ACTION: Strategic Architecture Actions Have the umbrella conversation with Dr. Sean. Not a pitch — a question: “Why are these ventures separate? What would it look like if they weren’t?” Let the answer guide the structure. Create an Edusky Education Group landing page. Even a single page on the existing website listing all service lines under one umbrella. This changes perception immediately. Develop an investor pitch deck. Show the group structure, the growth trajectory (Layer 0 through 5 progress), the market gap (neurodiverse-friendly, community-embedded, micro-school positioning), and the specific ask. Reactivate the language and training arms online. Add them to the website. Even basic pages signal that Edusky is more than one classroom. The “Smallest School” Reframe One final strategic note. The instinct is to hide the small student count. The smarter move is to own it. Globally, the micro-school movement is growing. Parents are actively seeking out small, personalised, relationship-driven alternatives to factory schooling. Edusky can position itself as an intentional micro-school: “We are small by design.” Even if the reality is more complicated, the positioning works because the educational outcome of smallness is genuinely better. Small class sizes mean every teacher knows every child by name, by personality, by learning style. Neurodiverse students — children with ADHD, autism spectrum, learning differences — are not rejected or warehoused. They are seen. This is a massively underserved market in JB. Parents of these children are desperate for schools that will work with them, not around them. The student production system (newspaper, podcast, social media, clubs) only works at this scale. A school of 500 cannot do what Edusky can do with 15 committed students who know each other and are known by every adult in the building. Community embeddedness — CSR, parent workshops, expert talks, student volunteers — is only credible at this scale. A mega-school running a charity event is a corporate CSR exercise. A micro-school running one is a community. The question is not whether Edusky can grow. It is whether the school is willing to do the unglamorous daily work of showing up: fixing the small things, telling its own story, and building trust one parent at a time. None of this requires money Edusky does not have. All of it requires consistency, documentation, and follow-through. The building blocks are already on the table. They just need assembling.
